About Me

Why computer science, and where I'm headed

A short professional biography covering why I chose IT, my career goals, and the skills I'm currently building.

01

Why Information Technology

I chose software development because I enjoy solving problems and understanding how things work. What interests me about programming is that you can start with a problem or an idea, break it down, write a solution, test it, and see the result for yourself. As I started learning Java and working with databases, I became more interested in what happens behind the software that people use every day. I wanted to understand how those systems are built rather than only being someone who uses them.

My studies have also shown me that software development requires more than just knowing how to write code. It involves logical thinking, patience, testing, problem-solving, and being willing to go back and figure out why something did not work.

02

Career goals

My current goal is to build a strong foundation in software development and gain enough practical experience to become a confident developer. At the moment, I am particularly interested in backend development and databases because I enjoy working with the logic and structure behind an application.

I am still open to exploring different areas of IT as I gain more experience. I don't want to choose a specialisation simply because it sounds good. I want to find an area that I genuinely enjoy and become very good at it. Longer term, I would like to work on real-world systems and applications where I can use my programming and problem-solving skills to build something useful.

03

Interests within IT

My main interests currently sit around application development and data. I enjoy working with Java, particularly object-oriented programming concepts such as classes, objects, inheritance, encapsulation, and methods. I am also interested in relational databases and understanding how information can be structured, connected, and managed effectively.

I find it interesting that there can be different ways to approach the same technical problem. Learning about relational and non-relational databases has helped me understand that the best solution depends on what the system actually needs rather than there always being one correct approach.
