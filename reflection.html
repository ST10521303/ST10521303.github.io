Professional Reflection

What this module has taught me

This section is intentionally left as a set of prompts — a reflection is meant to be in your own words, so fill each one in with your real experience, then delete these instructions.

This whole page needs to come from you. Below are the five questions your brief asks for, spaced out so you can write freely under each one. Aim for 300–500 words total across all five. Send me your rough answers (even messy bullet points) if you'd like help tightening the wording — I won't invent the substance for you.
  • 01 — What have you learned during this module?

    During this module, I learned how to use Java to build complete applications rather than just writing individual pieces of code. I became more comfortable working with classes, objects, methods, arrays, loops, conditional statements, user input, and input validation. One of the biggest things that clicked for me was understanding how different parts of a program can work together. Instead of putting everything into one large section of code, I learned to break a problem into smaller parts and give each part a specific purpose.

    Working on different projects also helped me understand that programming is a process. Sometimes my first solution did not work the way I expected, and I had to go back through my code, identify the problem, and make changes. This helped me become more comfortable with making mistakes and using them as part of the learning process rather than seeing an error as simply something going wrong.

  • 02 — Which IT professional skill is currently your strongest?

    I believe problem-solving is currently my strongest IT professional skill. When working on my projects, I often have to figure out why something is not working as expected and then work through the problem until I find a solution. I have learned not to immediately change random parts of my code, but instead to look at the problem logically and understand where it is coming from.

    For example, when working with user input and validation, I had to think about what could happen if a user entered information incorrectly. This meant making sure the program could handle unexpected input instead of assuming that everything would always be entered correctly. Experiences like this have helped me develop a more structured approach to solving programming problems.

  • 03 — Which skill would you like to improve?

    The skill I would most like to improve is my debugging and code-reading ability. I can usually understand what I want my program to do, but sometimes I take longer than I would like to identify exactly why something is not working. I want to become better at reading error messages, following the flow of my program, and identifying the root cause of a problem instead of only focusing on the part of the code where the error appears.

    I also want to become more confident working through difficult problems independently before looking for help. I think developing this skill will become increasingly important as my projects become more complicated.

  • 04 — What will you do to develop that skill?

    I plan to improve my debugging skills by regularly practising Java programming and building smaller projects outside of my normal assignments. Rather than immediately looking for a complete solution when I get stuck, I will spend more time understanding the error, testing different possibilities, and using debugging tools in NetBeans to follow what my program is actually doing.

    I also want to become more comfortable using official documentation and other reliable resources to understand how something works instead of simply copying a solution. My goal is to develop the ability to explain not only what I changed in my code, but also why the change fixed the problem.

  • 05 — How could this portfolio support your future studies and career?

    This portfolio can become a record of my development as I continue studying software development. I plan to keep updating it with projects, assignments, and other work that demonstrates the skills I gain over time. As my knowledge improves, I want the portfolio to show a clear progression from the projects I am working on now to more complex applications in the future.

    I also see the portfolio as a way to show potential employers more than just my qualification. It can give them examples of how I approach problems, how I structure my code, and how I have developed as a programmer. By keeping it updated throughout my studies, I hope it will eventually show not only what I have learned, but also what I am capable of building.
